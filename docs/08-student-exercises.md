# 8. Student exercises

## Exercise 1 — verify resonance

Calculate the resonant frequency for 30 microhenry and 82 nanofarad. Compare the
calculation with the simulated peak and explain any difference.

## Exercise 2 — coupling sweep

Run the model for coupling coefficients 0.02, 0.05, 0.10, 0.15 and 0.20. Plot
receiver RMS voltage and load power. Explain why coupling is not determined by
distance alone.

## Exercise 3 — detuning

Change the receiver capacitor by minus 10% and plus 10%. Determine how the peak
frequency and transferred power change.

## Exercise 4 — winding resistance

Repeat the sweep with coil ESR values of 0.1, 0.3, 0.6 and 1.0 ohm. Calculate
quality factor and compare it with peak load power.

## Exercise 5 — load variation

Test 5, 10, 20 and 50 ohm loads. Explain the relationship among receiver current,
load voltage, reflected impedance and efficiency.

## Exercise 6 — energy accounting

Calculate average source power, load power, source-resistor loss and coil losses.
Confirm that output power does not exceed input power.

## Exercise 7 — measurement plan

Prepare a safe laboratory procedure for measuring coil inductance, ESR, coupling,
resonant frequency and DC-to-DC efficiency. Identify required equipment and probe
ratings.

## Questions for discussion

1. Why does resonance not mean zero total input impedance in a loaded coupled system?
2. Why can resonant capacitor voltage exceed the source voltage?
3. Why is a fixed resistor an incomplete phone model?
4. What happens when a metal object is placed near the coils?
5. Which parameters should trigger transmitter shutdown?
